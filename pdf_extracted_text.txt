Image segmentation partitions an image into meaningful regions. In semantic segmen-

Background II: How Teacher–Student Learning Works
The idea
In teacher–student learning (also called knowledge distillation or model compression), a
large, accurate but expensive teacher model fT transfers what it has learned to a small,
cheap student model fS. The student is trained to reproduce the teacher’s outputs rather
than (or in addition to) the ground truth. The idea was introduced for compressing large
ensembles into small models [?] and popularised for neural networks [?]; it works equally
well for classical models. It is useful here for three reasons:
• Deployment cost. A 300-tree forest on 30 texture features, or a KNN that stores
50,000 reference pixels, is slow. A student on 6 cheap colour features can segment a
flood image in real time on a drone or phone.
• Label efficiency. The teacher is trained on only 40 labelled images, but it can label the
163 unlabelled images. The student therefore sees far more (pseudo-labelled) training
data than the teacher did.
• Regularisation. Teacher outputs are smoother and more consistent than hand-drawn
boundaries, which can make the student generalise better.
In this project the teacher and the student must always come from the same family: a
supervised teacher (KNN, RF or GNB) teaches a supervised student (KNN, RF or GNB),
and an unsupervised teacher (K-Means) teaches an unsupervised student (K-Means).
